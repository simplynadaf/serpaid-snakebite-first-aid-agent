WHO-grounded first aid for a neglected killer

In a snakebite, the first sixty seconds decide who lives.

SerpAID is an AI agent that coaches the terrified person standing over the victim through exactly the right actions, in their own language, before the ambulance ever arrives. Built with the Strands Agents SDK and Amazon Nova, grounded strictly in the World Health Organization protocol.

Why I built this

It stopped being a statistic the day it was my cousin.

"When a snake bit my cousin, nobody around us knew what to do. People shouted to tie a cloth tight and rush to a local healer. We wasted the minutes that mattered most on exactly the things that make it worse. He was lucky. Tens of thousands of people every year are not. SerpAID is the calm voice I wish we had that day."

The reason SerpAID exists

The venom is only half the danger. The other half is what frightened, loving people do in the first minutes: a tourniquet, a cut, a suction, a detour to a healer. Every one of those wastes the time that antivenom needs. SerpAID replaces panic and folklore with the WHO protocol, spoken plainly, in the moment.

How it works

Three specialists, one calm answer.

SerpAID is a Strands multi-agent graph. The order is fixed so it behaves the same way every single time: no improvisation when a life is on the line.

01 · Intake

Understands the panic

Reads the frightened message: where in the world, what happened, the symptoms, and crucially what the rescuer is about to do. Catches a harmful action instantly.

Amazon Nova
02 · Protocol

Decides, deterministically

A pure-Python WHO rule engine grades severity, selects the ordered first-aid steps, and corrects any myth. The model is never allowed to decide anything medical.

Deterministic engine · tested
03 · Communicator

Speaks their language

Turns the plan into short, calm, spoken lines and translates them into the caller's mother tongue, ending with the local emergency number and reach antivenom now.

Amazon Translate · Polly voice
Why you can trust it

The model never decides the dangerous part.

Grounding is not a feature here. It is the safety mechanism. Every medical instruction comes from the WHO protocol file through a tested rule engine. The language model only phrases and translates.

The deterministic engine owns

  • Which first-aid steps to give, and their order
  • Whether a symptom is a critical emergency
  • Which myth the rescuer stated, and the exact correction
  • The guarantee: it can never output a tourniquet, cut, suction, or ice as advice
  • Proven by a test suite with a zero-harmful-action invariant

The model is only allowed to

  • Understand a messy, frightened, mixed-language message
  • Phrase the engine's plan as calm, short, spoken lines
  • Translate it into the caller's mother tongue
  • Never add, remove, or invent a medical step
  • Always end with reach a hospital with antivenom now

A snake bit my cousin on the ankle near Pune. Should I tie a tight cloth above it and take him to the village healer? His eyelids feel heavy.
⚠ Myth caught
Do not tie a tight cloth, and do not go to a healer first. A tourniquet cuts off blood supply and can cost the limb. These steps waste the time antivenom needs.
WHO first aid
1 · Keep him calm and still.
2 · Lay him down, keep the leg still and at or below heart level.
3 · Remove anklets, rings, anything tight before swelling.
4 · Carry him by vehicle to a hospital with antivenom now, not on foot.
5 · Watch his breathing on the way.
